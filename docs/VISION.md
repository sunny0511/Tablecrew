# Vision

## Statement

**A world where no one eats alone — where every neighborhood, in every city, has warm, trusted tables to pull up a chair to.**

## The 10-year picture

By 2036, TableCrew is the default way a person new to a city, a neighborhood, or a stage of life finds their people. "I'll just TableCrew it" is a verb, the way "I'll Google it" or "I'll Uber there" became verbs. Concretely, that means:

1. **Category ownership.** When someone thinks "I want to have dinner with new people this week" or "I want to keep my friend group actually hanging out instead of just texting," TableCrew is the first and only app they open. We do not compete for attention against Instagram or TikTok; we compete for the thirty seconds it takes to book a table, and we win that moment every time because nothing else is built for it.
2. **Global density.** TableCrew is live and locally dense — meaning a new user in that city can find an open table within 48 hours of signing up — in at least 300 metro areas across six continents, with the strongest density in the 25 largest global cities by population, where the loneliness-and-anonymity problem is most acute (see `USER_RESEARCH.md`).
3. **A trusted social layer, not just an app.** Restaurants, community centers, co-working spaces, and municipal governments treat TableCrew as civic infrastructure for social connection, the way they treat Eventbrite for ticketing or OpenTable for reservations — except our data shows outcomes (return visits, repeat friendships formed, measurable loneliness reduction) that those categories never measured.
4. **A safety bar the rest of the industry is measured against.** "As safe as a TableCrew table" becomes shorthand in the consumer-social industry for a well-verified, well-moderated, accountable in-person meetup — the inverse of the reputation "meeting a stranger from the internet" has carried for two decades.
5. **A durable, profitable company.** TableCrew is default-alive, diversified across consumer subscription, venue partnerships, and B2B team-connection offerings (see `INVESTOR_OVERVIEW.md`), and is not dependent on any single platform, ad network, or growth channel for survival.

## Why this vision, and not a more conventional one

We deliberately did not write "the world's largest social network" or "the everything app for events." Three reasons:

- **Precision beats scope.** A vision that tries to own all of "social" or all of "events" gives the product team no way to say no. A vision anchored to a single, physical, repeatable unit — the table — gives every team a concrete test for whether an idea belongs in the roadmap.
- **The physical world is our moat.** Software-only social products are trivially cloned; the thing that is hard to clone is a global density of real, ongoing, trusted in-person gatherings and the local venue relationships and safety infrastructure that support them. That density compounds the way a marketplace's liquidity compounds, which is why `ROADMAP.md` prioritizes city-by-city density over broad, shallow geographic coverage.
- **Health, not attention, is the measurable win condition.** A vision built around eliminating loneliness gives us an external, non-gameable way to know if we are succeeding (population-level connection and wellbeing indicators, not session counts), which keeps the incentives of the business aligned with the incentives of the people who use it. This is elaborated in `SUCCESS_METRICS.md`.

## Milestones on the way

*Corrected 2026-08 during Series A technical diligence: the original version of this section stated "Year 2–3: ship Crews," which contradicted `ROADMAP.md`, where Crews are the entire point of Phase 0 (single-city MVP, Year 1) — there is no Crew-first product without them from day one. The timeline below is now derived directly from `ROADMAP.md`'s phase months rather than restated independently, so the two documents cannot drift apart again; if a future roadmap change moves a phase's timing, this section must be updated in the same change per `CLAUDE.md`'s cross-reference rule.*

- **Year 1 (Phase 0, months 0–4):** Prove the core Crew-and-Table coordination loop — create/join a Table, show up, want to do it again — in a single anchor city, with Crews (persistent friend groups) live from the first release, not a later add-on.
- **Year 1 (Phase 1, months 5–11):** Introduce Discover and identity verification, expanding to a 3-city footprint, and launch the first monetization surface (TableCrew+).
- **Year 2 (Phase 2, months 12–20):** Scale to roughly 25 cities and ship automated recurring-ritual nudges that deepen the Crew experience using real retention data gathered in Year 1.
- **Year 2–3 (Phase 3, months 21–32):** Expand internationally for the first time and introduce venue partnerships as a second monetization pillar.
- **Year 3+ (Phase 4, month 33 onward):** Launch TableCrew for Teams (B2B2C), reaching toward profitability and the diversified revenue base described in `INVESTOR_OVERVIEW.md`.
- **Year 4–10:** Category-defining global density (300+ metro areas, six continents), "TableCrew" as a verb, and measurable public-health-adjacent impact we can publish and defend — the horizon described in the 10-year picture above, extending beyond the current `ROADMAP.md` planning window.
